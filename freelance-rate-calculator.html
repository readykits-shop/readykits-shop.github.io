Freelance Rate Calculator: How Much Should You Charge?

Many new freelancers take their old salary, divide it by 2,080 hours and undercharge badly. As a freelancer you pay your own taxes and expenses, take unpaid time off and spend a lot of hours on non-billable work. Here's the formula that accounts for all of that.

The formula

Minimum hourly rate = (Target income + Business expenses) ÷ (1 − Tax set-aside %) ÷ Billable hours per year

Where billable hours per year = (52 − weeks off) × billable hours per week.

Worked example

Input Value
Target take-home income $60,000
Business expenses (software, equipment, insurance) $6,000
Tax set-aside 28%
Weeks off (vacation, holidays, sick) 6
Billable hours per week 25
  • Gross revenue needed: ($60,000 + $6,000) ÷ 0.72 = $91,667
  • Billable hours: (52 − 6) × 25 = 1,150
  • Minimum rate: $91,667 ÷ 1,150 ≈ $80/hour
  • Day rate (6 billable hours): ≈ $478

The same income as an employee looks like $29/hour on paper. The real freelance number is nearly three times that.

Why 25 billable hours, not 40?

Sales calls, proposals, invoicing, admin and learning eat 30–40% of a full-time freelancer's week. If you plan for 40 billable hours, you'll miss your income target.

Why set aside 25–30% for taxes?

US freelancers pay self-employment tax (15.3% Social Security + Medicare) on top of income tax, and usually pay quarterly estimated taxes (Apr 15, Jun 15, Sep 15, Jan 15). Your exact rate depends on your income and state, so check with a tax professional. (This is general information, not tax advice.)

From hourly to project pricing

Estimate the hours, multiply by your rate and add a 10–20% buffer for revisions and unknowns. Then quote the project price, not the hours. Clients compare outcomes, not timesheets.
