What every freelance invoice needs

  1. Invoice number. Use sequential numbers (INV-0001, INV-0002…). Your bookkeeping and your client's accounts-payable team both depend on them.
  2. Issue date and due date. Write the real date ("Due: March 14"). "Net 14" alone is easy to miss.
  3. Your details. Business name, address, email and, if you have one, your EIN or business tax ID. If you're in the US, give clients an EIN on your W-9 instead of your Social Security number. An EIN is free from IRS.gov.
  4. Client details. The legal company name and the billing contact. Ask for these during onboarding.
  5. Itemized services. One line per deliverable or block of hours, with quantity, rate and amount. "Website work, $2,000" invites questions. "Homepage design (1) $1,200 + 2 landing pages (2 × $400)" doesn't.
  6. Subtotal, deposit received, tax and total due. If you took a deposit, subtract it on the invoice so the balance is clear.
  7. Payment methods. ACH or bank details plus a card or PayPal/Stripe link. Every extra step slows payment down.
  8. Late fee terms. For example "1.5% per month on overdue balances", and only if your contract says the same.

Payment terms that work

  • New clients: take a 30–50% deposit before you start. Invoice the balance on delivery with Net 7 or Net 14.
  • Ongoing retainers: invoice at the start of each month, not the end.
  • Large projects: bill at milestones (for example 40/30/30) instead of one big final invoice.

A simple follow-up schedule

When What to send
Day 0 Invoice + payment link
3 days before due "Friendly heads-up, invoice INV-0012 is due Friday."
Day 1 overdue Polite reminder with the invoice re-attached
Day 7 overdue Reminder that the late fee now applies; work pauses until it's paid

A tracker that counts overdue days for you makes this automatic. You open one sheet on Monday and see who to nudge.

Do I charge sales tax or VAT?

In the US, most freelance services aren't subject to sales tax, but the rules vary by state and by service type. Check with your state. If you invoice an EU business from another EU country, the invoice usually needs "reverse charge" wording and 0% VAT. Our kit includes an optional template for that.
