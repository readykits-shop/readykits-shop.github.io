How to Write a Freelance Proposal That Gets a Yes

Most proposals lose because they're about the freelancer: your process, your tools, your experience. The ones that win are about the client's problem and make saying yes easy. Here's a one-page structure you can reuse.

1. Open with their goal, in their words

Two or three sentences that restate what they told you on the call:

"You want to cut onboarding emails from 40 a week to under 10 by launching a self-serve help center before your March product launch."

If they read that and think "exactly," you've already beaten most competing proposals.

2. Offer three options, not one price

One price gives the client a yes/no choice. Three options turn it into which one.

  • Basic: the minimum that solves the core problem.
  • Standard (recommended): what you actually think they need. Mark it as recommended.
  • Premium: Standard plus speed, extra deliverables or ongoing support.

Most clients pick the middle option. Price Basic so Standard looks like the obvious value.

3. Spell out scope, and what's out of scope

List the deliverables with formats and quantities. Then add one line of exclusions ("Copywriting and stock photos not included"). That one line prevents most scope creep.

4. Timeline with a dependency

"First draft in 10 business days after we receive your brand assets. Final delivery 5 days after feedback." Tying deadlines to their input protects you.

5. Terms in one paragraph

Deposit (30–50%), payment due dates, number of revision rounds and the rate for extra rounds. Refer to the contract you'll send next.

6. One clear next step and an expiry date

"Reply with the option you'd like and I'll send the agreement and deposit invoice. This proposal is valid until [date]." An expiry date gently prompts a decision.

Common mistakes

  • Sending a 10-page PDF when one page would do.
  • Quoting hourly when the client cares about outcomes.
  • No deadline, so the proposal sits in their inbox forever.
  • Starting work before the contract is signed and the deposit is paid.
